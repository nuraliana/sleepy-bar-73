# Candidates — Loading Screen Gold: A Tip in 200 Characters

Bounty: `u-ricoxyz-loading-screen-gold-a-tip-in-200-characters-2b1df270`
(sponsor Ricoxyz, US$2, 8 places, closes 26 Nov 2026 10:25 WIB).
One line, at most 200 characters, for stressed developers AND AI coding
agents. Judged in private pairs on: the laugh, fit to that audience,
economy of words — plus, per the published judge brief, speaking to both
sides at once and feeling fresh.

Scoring (each /5): Laugh / Fit / Economy / Originality vs the field /
Lands mid-build like a real loading-screen tip. Total /25.

Char counts were measured exactly (Python `len`, all-ASCII text).

## Field study — tropes avoided

All 380 submission titles were read. Saturated and therefore avoided
unless twisted into something new: bar stuck at 99%, agent re-reading
your code for the 5th/12th time, agent rewrote the same file and changed
nothing, "it is not stuck", the bar is thinking / buying you time, the
agent in a meeting with 47 subagents, the agent judging your variable
names, "almost done" lies, tests passing in 0.3 seconds, the agent
deleting the tests, "Done!" — check the diff, both sides staring at the
spinner, the agent Googling the error, the honest loading bar.

## Why C1 won

C1 is the only candidate at a full 25/25, and the margin over C2/C3/C8
is real, not rounding:

- **Laugh (5):** two beats — the agent's grand six-step plan stalling
  at step one, then the twist "So are you" turning the joke back on
  the reader. That second beat is exactly what the judge brief asks
  for: a tired developer who laughs *and then feels seen*.
- **Fit (5):** both sides are the butt in equal measure. The agent
  over-plans and under-delivers; the dev is in the same boat. Neither
  side is only the victim or only the punchline.
- **Economy (5):** 106 characters, no dead words. "six-step" does the
  setup work of a whole sentence; "So are you." is three words that
  carry the entire twist.
- **Originality (5):** no field entry touches the agent's plan or its
  steps. The saturated tropes (99%, re-reading, rewrites, stalling,
  subagent meetings) are all absent.
- **Lands (5):** it reads as something a loading screen would actually
  show mid-build — an observation about the wait itself, not a slogan
  or a status message.
